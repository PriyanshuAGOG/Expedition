What is this
expedition?

A medically reviewed preparation programme and supported Himalayan field experience for adults living with Type 2 diabetes—followed by a public archive of responsible, anonymised learning.

01 01 · The purpose

Why are we doing this?

World Diabetes Day often creates awareness for one day. This programme asks a longer question: what becomes possible when medical review, daily habits, progressive movement and community support are practised consistently?

Diabetes should not make life smaller.
02 02 · What it is

A complete programme—not simply a trek.

  • Multi-stage application and medical screening
  • Sixty days of structured preparation
  • A supported 5–7 day Himalayan expedition
  • Post-expedition research and public learning
03 03 · Starting eligibility

Who should consider applying?

  • Adults living with Type 2 diabetes
  • Applicants from across India
  • Ready to commit about one hour each morning
  • Open to screening, guidance and group accountability
  • Available around 14 November 2026
No previous trekking experience is required. Application does not guarantee selection.

Before you apply

Clear answers.
No summit promises.

Do I need previous trekking experience?+

No. The planned route is simple to moderate, and the 60-day phase builds readiness. Final participation still depends on medical and fitness clearance.

Does applying guarantee a place?+

No. Approximately 15 people will be selected through multiple stages based on medical safety, readiness, commitment and group fit.

Can I stop my diabetes medicine?+

No. Never stop or change diabetes medication or insulin without your treating doctor's direction. NirogBhumi does not replace your doctor.

What happens during the 60 days?+

Participants follow an approximately one-hour morning routine supported by health review, movement, nutrition and habits, recovery, stress regulation and weekly accountability.

Will my health information be published?+

Not as individual medical data. Public findings will be anonymised and aggregated. Personal stories or identifiable media require separate explicit consent.
